# Causality Assessment of Adverse Drug Reactions

## Introduction
Causality assessment is the systematic evaluation of the relationship between a suspected drug and an adverse drug reaction (ADR).

## Standard Causality Categories

### 1. Certain
There is a clear temporal relationship between drug administration and the reaction. Other possible causes are excluded. The reaction improves after withdrawal of the drug and may recur on re-exposure.

### 2. Probable
There is a reasonable time relationship between the drug and reaction. Alternative causes are unlikely, and the reaction improves after withdrawal of the suspected drug.

### 3. Possible
There is a reasonable time relationship between drug administration and the reaction, but other drugs or diseases could also explain the reaction.

## Case Study 1 – Certain
A patient develops anaphylaxis shortly after receiving an antibiotic. The antibiotic is stopped and the symptoms resolve. On accidental re-exposure, the same reaction occurs.

**Assessment: Certain**

**Reason:** Strong temporal relationship, positive dechallenge and positive rechallenge.

## Case Study 2 – Probable
A patient develops a skin rash five days after starting a new medicine. No other new medicines were started. The medicine is discontinued and the rash disappears.

**Assessment: Probable**

**Reason:** Appropriate timing, no convincing alternative cause, and improvement after withdrawal.

## Case Study 3 – Possible
A patient taking a drug develops nausea. However, the patient also has a gastrointestinal illness that can cause nausea. There is insufficient information after withdrawal of the drug.

**Assessment: Possible**

**Reason:** The timing is compatible with an ADR, but an alternative cause is present.

## Factors Considered in Causality Assessment

1. Time relationship between drug administration and reaction.
2. Dechallenge – improvement after stopping the drug.
3. Rechallenge – recurrence after restarting the drug.
4. Presence of alternative causes.
5. Known pharmacological or clinical pattern of the reaction.
6. Dose-response relationship.

## Conclusion
Causality assessment helps determine how strongly an adverse reaction is associated with a suspected drug. The categories Certain, Probable and Possible are assigned according to the strength of evidence and the presence of alternative explanations.
